# ADR-0047: Time: a document's life in formulas, what is due, and timed transitions

- Status: Accepted; amends ADR-0008 (a transition may fire by time), ADR-0013 (three more system variables), ADR-0014 (three more system columns; engine schema 5), ADR-0018 (`after` and `at` on a transition), ADR-0019 (the functions of a document's life), ADR-0040 (what entering a state is, and the `_states` view), ADR-0044 (the wake-up row)
- Date: 2026-11-11

## Context

The owner ruled on 2026-11-10 that Phase 4 builds time in two halves,
the first before the second (ADR-0044, "Time"):

1. *Conditions on time*: whether a document could, or should, move from
   one state to another.
2. *Timers and a scheduler*: firing a transition at a given time, with
   its retries.

What runs today. A guard is a formula and may read `TODAY()`, `NOW()`
and any date the document holds, so `TODAY() > due` can be written.
`Transitions` says whether a document could move, at the database's
clock or at a moment given with `At` (ADR-0042). An eventless transition
with such a guard says that it should, and it moves when a commit
touches the document, not when the time comes.

What is missing.

- A formula reads nothing of the document's own life. It has `STATE()`
  and `ID()`. It cannot say "seven days in this state", "the person who
  created it may not approve it" or "back to whoever changed it last".
  The facts are stored: every version keeps who made it, when and in
  which state, and the `_states` view lists every stay in a state. No
  function reaches them.
- Nothing lists the documents that would move now. A caller asks
  document by document.
- Nothing fires by itself.

The owner gave two things on 2026-11-11. They are items 1 and 2. After
reading the record he added a third, which is in items 3 and 6: every
entry into a state is recorded, and it can be asked when a document
first entered a state and by whom, and when it last left it.

## Decision

1. **A timer is written on a transition** (the owner's ruling). A timer
   always moves the document. A reminder is a move too: a transition
   from a state back into itself, with the reminder as the action on it.
   A rule that fires by time and leaves the document where it is is not
   built.

2. **A formula reads the life of its document** (the owner's
   requirement): when and by whom it was created and last changed, when
   and by whom it entered the state it is in, and for any state when and
   by whom it first and last entered it and left it, how often and how
   long it was there, and who first and last changed it there. Items 3 to 7
   are that family. Process mining reads the same facts across all
   documents from the views, so a function and a view give one answer.

### The first half: checking

3. **What entering a state is.** A document enters a state when it is
   created in it and when a transition lands in it, named or eventless,
   from another state or from the same one. Each entry begins a stay,
   and ends the stay before it: a transition into the same state leaves
   it and enters it at one moment.

   **Every entry is recorded** (the owner's requirement), in three
   places that say the same. The version the entry makes is a row of the
   history, with the state, the moment and who made it. The log has its
   event, `created` or `transition`, with the state left and the state
   entered. And the `_states` view has one row for the stay it begins.
   ADR-0040, item 7, began a visit only where the state differs from
   the previous version's, so a return into the same state was folded
   into the stay before it; the view now has a row for every entry. It
   gains `entered_by` and `left_by`, who made the version that began
   the stay and who made the one that ended it. Nothing of this is ever
   changed or removed: the history and the log are append-only
   (ADR-0006).

4. **The document keeps who.** Three system columns join the seven:
   `created_by`, `updated_by` and `state_entered_by`, the principal of
   the commit that created the document, made its current version, and
   began its current stay. They are blank when that commit's command
   named no principal. What the outbox does it does as the person whose
   command queued it (ADR-0044), so that person is who. They are
   columns of the document table, of its history, of the views and of
   the class as a `LOOKUP` reads it (ADR-0046), and fields of the wire
   document. The names are reserved. The engine schema is 5.

5. **The functions of the row.** They take no argument and cost nothing:
   the commit has the row in hand.

   | Function | Answers | Type |
   |---|---|---|
   | `CREATEDAT()`, `CREATEDBY()` | when the document was created, and by whom | datetime, text |
   | `UPDATEDAT()`, `UPDATEDBY()` | when its current version was made, and by whom | datetime, text |
   | `ENTEREDAT()`, `ENTEREDBY()` | when it entered the state it is in, and who moved it there | datetime, text |
   | `VERSION()` | its version | number |

   "Seven days in this state" is `NOW() >= ADDDAYS(ENTEREDAT(), 7)`.
   The owner's `CurrentEditor` is `USER()`, which exists.

6. **The functions of a state.** They take the name of a state. A name
   written as text is checked against the class's states when the class
   is defined.

   | Function | Answers | Type |
   |---|---|---|
   | `FIRSTENTERED(state)`, `FIRSTENTEREDBY(state)` | when the document first entered the state, and who moved it there; blank if it never did | datetime, text |
   | `LASTENTERED(state)`, `LASTENTEREDBY(state)` | the same of its last entry | datetime, text |
   | `FIRSTLEFT(state)`, `FIRSTLEFTBY(state)` | when it first left the state, and who moved it out; blank if it never left | datetime, text |
   | `LASTLEFT(state)`, `LASTLEFTBY(state)` | the same of the last time it left; for a document that is in the state, the time before | datetime, text |
   | `ENTRIES(state)` | how many times it entered it | number |
   | `DAYSIN(state)` | the days it has spent there in all, with fractions, up to now when it is there | number |
   | `FIRSTEDITOR(state)`, `LASTEDITOR(state)` | who made the first and the last version of the document while it was in the state, versions made with no principal left out | text |

   They read the document's own events and versions, once per command
   however many of them a command evaluates, through the index the
   event log already has. They see the document's life up to the commit
   being computed, the entry that commit makes included.

7. **Where they may be written.** They are context functions, like
   `STATE()` and `ID()`: in a default, a triggered variable, a guard, a
   rule, an access rule and a right, and not in an auto formula, whose
   value must follow from the document's own values (ADR-0015). They
   speak of the document the formula belongs to. Another document is
   read with `LOOKUP`, where `created_by`, `updated_by`,
   `state_entered_by` and the three moments are columns.

8. **What is due.** `Due(class)` lists the documents of a class that
   would move if a commit touched them now, or at the moment given with
   `At`: each with its state, the state it would land in, and the moment
   since which that has been so when the engine knows it. It evaluates
   the guards of the eventless and the timed transitions as one filter
   per state where the guards translate to SQL (ADR-0038), and document
   by document where they do not. `Transitions` shows a timed transition
   with the moment it fires.

### The second half: firing

9. **`after` and `at`.** A transition may carry one of them:

   ```json
   {"from": "sent", "to": "overdue", "after": "30d"}
   {"from": "sent", "to": "overdue", "at": "ADDDAYS(due, 1)"}
   {"name": "remind", "from": "waiting", "to": "waiting", "after": "3d"}
   ```

   `after` is a length of time in the state: a whole number and `d`,
   `h`, `m` or `s`. A day is 24 hours. `at` is a formula over the
   document's own values and the functions of items 5 and 6, giving a
   date, which means the first instant of that day in UTC, or a
   datetime; it may not read the clock or the user. A blank `at` never
   comes. A guard beside either is still asked.

10. **A timed transition is a condition on time that the engine
    promises to look at.** Its meaning is that of an eventless
    transition whose guard also says "the time has come": it fires in
    the chain of any commit made once the time has come (ADR-0040).
    What the engine adds is the commit: when a document enters the
    state, the same transaction queues a wake-up row for each timed
    transition that leaves it, due at its moment (ADR-0044, item 1).
    So a timer that the worker missed fires at the next commit, and a
    stopped worker loses nothing.

11. **The wake-up.** When it is due the worker looks at the document.
    If it is gone, in another state or in a later stay, or the moment
    has moved to later, the row is done and writes no event. Otherwise
    it commits, and the timed transition fires in that commit's chain.
    A failure is retried like any row, and a row that is dead is listed
    like any dead action. When a commit changes a value that an `at`
    reads, it queues a row for the new moment; the old one finds nothing
    to do.

12. **Who acts.** The wake-up carries the principal of the commit that
    began the stay, as ADR-0044, item 11, has it for every row. The move
    is made as that person: `USER()` in its rules is theirs, and what
    those rules do to other documents is done with their rights. Its
    event names them as actor, the event that began the stay as cause,
    and the row's key. The move itself asks no right: it is the
    document's own transition, and a guard is the only thing that stops
    one.

13. **A name on a timed transition** lets a rule hang on it, as on
    `remind` above. It also makes it a named transition like any other:
    a person may take it before its time, under its guard and its
    access rule.

14. **What is refused when the class is defined.** Both `after` and
    `at` on one transition. A timed transition out of a terminal state.
    An `at` on a transition into its own state, which would fire again
    at once: `after` is how a stay repeats. An `after` that is not a
    length of time. An `at` that reads the clock, the user or another
    document.

15. **The clock is the database's.** A timer fires by the clock a
    commit runs by (ADR-0042). A simulation is a copy of the file
    opened with `Options.Now` and a `Drain`.

16. **What waits.** A rule that fires by time without moving the
    document. `schedule` on a rule and on a variable, which recompute
    many documents at once and belong to the analytical engine
    (ADR-0044). Working days and time zones: a day is 24 hours of UTC.
    A length of time as a type of the formula language.

17. **The conformance suite.** `conformance/formula/context.json` gains
    the functions of items 5 and 6, with the life of a document given
    inline. `conformance/statechart/` gains the entry into the same
    state and the `_states` view. `conformance/rules/` gains `timers`,
    scripts that move the clock: a transition after a time, at a date
    the document holds, a reminder that repeats, a date that is changed
    while the document waits, a document that leaves before its time,
    and a worker that was stopped.

## Consequences

- **Four eyes, and the like, are one line.** The access rule of
  `approve` says `CREATEDBY() <> USER()`. A purchase goes back to
  `LASTEDITOR("draft")`. An invoice that was `sent` three times says so
  with `ENTRIES("sent")`. Who first approved it is
  `FIRSTENTEREDBY("approved")`, and when it last came out of review is
  `LASTLEFT("review")`.
- **Mining and formulas read one set of facts.** `Invoice_states` has
  every stay with who began it and who ended it; the functions answer
  the same for one document. A transition back into the same state is a stay of its own
  in both, so a reminder shows as a step.
- **A database made before this record does not open**, as with every
  change of the engine schema before 1.0: there is no upgrade path, and
  the database is made again. The three columns could have been filled
  from the history, since every version kept its actor. The owner said
  on 2026-11-11 that no database exists yet, so the standing rule
  stays.
- **`created_by`, `updated_by` and `state_entered_by` are reserved
  names.** A class with a variable of one of those names is refused.
- **A timer is evidence like any move.** The log shows who began the
  stay, that the time came, and what the move did. It shows the move as
  that person's, with the cause beside it. Whoever reads the log for
  what a person did at a moment has to read the cause too.
- **A reminder restarts its own state's timers.** The transition back
  into `waiting` begins a new stay, so every `after` out of `waiting`
  counts again from it. An escalation that must not be pushed back by
  reminders is written with `at`, from a moment that does not move:
  `ADDDAYS(FIRSTENTERED("waiting"), 14)`.
- **Time costs a row.** A document that enters a state with timed
  transitions queues one row for each. A class without them queues
  nothing.

## Performance

- The functions of the row are free: no allocation, as `STATE()` and
  `ID()`. The benchmarks of the gate keep their allocations, and a class
  that uses no function of items 5 and 6 does what it did.
- The functions of a state cost one read of the document's own events
  per command: under 100 µs for a document with 100 versions, the cold
  budget of ADR-0016. A benchmark in the `store` package proves it.
- A commit that queues one wake-up stays inside the 2 ms of a
  transition commit (ADR-0006, ADR-0012). A wake-up that finds nothing
  to do is one read and one update.
- `Due` over a class of 100,000 documents, of which 100 are due, with a
  guard that compares an indexed variable: within the indexed-filter
  budget of ADR-0012. The last step of the phase measures it, and the
  rate at which timers fire, and `docs/perf/v0.4.0.md` reports both.
