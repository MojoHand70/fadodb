# v0.4.1: many documents moved by one call

Measured on 2026-11-13 on main after #79, with Go 1.26.11, linux/amd64
and SQLite 1.62.0, on an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX with 24 threads and
31 GB available to the Linux VM (WSL2, kernel 6.18), the database files
on the virtual disk that holds the home directory, ext4 on a VHD on
NVMe. An fsync of 4 KB cost 1.1 ms at the median and 1.5 ms at p90 that
day, measured with 200 writes and fsyncs of one block from Python; on
the day of [v0.4.0](v0.4.0.md) it cost 1.0 ms and 1.8 ms. The in-memory
benchmarks are the mean of three runs; the campaign ran once and
reports percentiles. The budgets are those of
[ADR-0048](../adr/0048-moving-many-documents-in-one-command.md), with
the rows of the commit campaign run again on the same build.

This release adds one thing to v0.4.0: `TransitionMany`, a call that
moves many documents of a class along one transition in one
transaction, a commit each, and `PreviewMany`, which tries the batch
and writes nothing. Every budget holds. One thing is slower than a
reader might expect and has no budget; it is named under
[Another writer while batches run](#another-writer-while-batches-run).

## The budgets of ADR-0048

| Measure | Budget | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Documents moved a second, a plain transition in batches of 1,000, on disk | ≥ 15,000 | 27,257 over 1,000,000 documents |
| A document that is refused | costs no more than one that moves | 5.8 µs and 83 allocations against 36.3 µs and 147 (`BenchmarkTransitionManyRefused`, `BenchmarkTransitionMany`) |
| The mark set before each document, by itself | published, no budget | 0.47 µs and 6 allocations (`BenchmarkSavepoint`) |
| The rate of other writers while batches run | published, no budget | 99 single sets a second while batches ran back to back; see below |

The record expected about 25,000 documents a second, from what the
batched create does against the single one, and set the budget under
it.

## A million documents moved from one state to another

Each move is still a commit of its own, with its guard, its rights, its
rules, its version, its history row and its event. Only the
transaction is shared.

| What moves the documents | Rate | A million documents |
|---|---|---|
| a command, `Transition` for each | 12,427 a second | 80 seconds |
| `TransitionMany`, 1,000 documents a call | 27,257 a second | 37 seconds |

A call of 1,000 documents takes 35.3 ms at the median, 37.2 ms at p90
and 49.4 ms at p99. On the day of v0.4.0 a command each took 87
seconds.

## In memory

`-benchmem`, 1,000 documents an iteration for the three benchmarks of
the batch, given here for one document:

| Benchmark | Time a document | Allocations a document |
|---|---|---|
| `BenchmarkTransition`, a command each, for comparison | 54.7 µs | 155 |
| `BenchmarkTransitionMany` | 36.3 µs | 147 |
| `BenchmarkPreviewMany` | 30.7 µs | 141 |
| `BenchmarkTransitionManyRefused`, a document whose guard is FALSE | 5.8 µs | 83 |
| `BenchmarkSavepoint`, the mark alone | 0.47 µs | 6 |

A move in a batch costs two thirds of a move by its own command. What
it saves is the transaction: the begin, the commit and the lock, once
for the batch. `BenchmarkTransitionMany` is in the benchmark gate, so a
change that slows the batch or adds an allocation to it fails CI.

## Another writer while batches run

One batch holds the writer for its length, 35 ms for 1,000 documents.
The campaign measured what that costs somebody else: 1,000 single sets
were made, one after another, while batches of 1,000 ran back to back
with no pause between them and moved 265,000 documents.

| The single set | Waited |
|---|---|
| half of them | not at all: 0.15 ms |
| one in ten | two batches: 73 ms |
| one in a hundred | 77 ms |
| the slowest | 120 ms |

A commit that arrives during a batch waits for it to end, and it may
wait for the next one as well, since the writer's lock is not handed
over in the order of arrival. Nobody waited longer than 120 ms, and no
commit failed. A caller who moves many documents while people work can
leave a pause between two calls, or send smaller batches. A lock that
is handed over in the order of arrival would bound the wait at one
batch; it would touch every commit and is not built.

## How to run it

```
FADODB_BENCH_DIR=/path/on/disk FADODB_BENCH_COMMITS=1000000 go test -count=1 -timeout 2h -v -run TestCommitCampaign ./internal/store/
go test -run '^$' -bench '^Benchmark(Transition|TransitionMany|TransitionManyRefused|PreviewMany|Savepoint)$' -benchmem -count=3 ./internal/store/
```

The campaign took 15 minutes. Point `FADODB_BENCH_DIR` at the disk the
numbers are meant for, never at `/tmp`, as
[the index of this directory](README.md) says.
