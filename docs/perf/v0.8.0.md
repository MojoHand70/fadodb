# v0.8.0: the analytical engine, Phase 8

Measured on 2026-11-28 between one and a quarter past five in the
morning on main at `66b3124`, which holds every pull request of the
phase but #288, the fix this measuring found; the rows #288 changes were
measured again on its branch, beside main, between 06:22 and 07:52. With
Go 1.26.11, linux/amd64, SQLite 1.62.0 and DuckDB 2.0.0, on an Intel
Core Ultra 9 275HX with 24 threads and 31 GB available to the Linux VM
(WSL2, kernel 6.18), the database files on the virtual disk that holds
the home directory, ext4 on a VHD on NVMe. An fsync of 4 KB cost 0.48 ms
at the median when the run began at 00:58, 4.07 ms at 01:51, 0.46 ms at
03:52 and 3.74 ms at 05:08, when the last campaign on disk ended. A
commit does not wait for the disk unless the database was created with
`FULL`. The in-memory benchmarks are the mean of three runs. The
campaign of the server ran three times, and its table is of the first.
Each question over the large database was asked five times of the
program with the copy and three of the one without, and the middle
answer is given; each such program ran three times where a row says so.
The others ran once and report percentiles. A demo that the owner uses
served a database of its own on the machine throughout, and no other
measuring ran. The budgets are those of
[ADR-0017](../adr/0017-duckdb-as-the-analytical-engine.md),
[ADR-0075](../adr/0075-the-engine-makes-its-own-indexes.md),
[ADR-0085](../adr/0085-lists-in-and-out.md),
[ADR-0089](../adr/0089-the-copy-beside-the-documents.md) and
[ADR-0090](../adr/0090-totals-by-group-in-a-query.md), and the owner's
bar for a value that follows a table
([ADR-0091](../adr/0091-a-kept-value-that-follows-its-table.md)), with
the rows of Phases 2 to 7 run again on the same build.

Every budget of Phase 8 holds: over ten million moves the copy answers
which state follows which, with the spread of the stays, in 86 ms of the
2 s allowed, and a change of a table brings 100,000 documents in about
4.1 s of the owner's 5. One budget of Phase 5 failed: a read rule that
reads the row cost an indexed filter 250% where 15% is allowed, through
the way Phase 8 chooses between two indexes. #288 mends most of it, and
the 43 to 46% left are open, for the owner. Measured beside v0.7.0 the
same night, every commit is 8 to 10% slower in memory and 10 to 16%
slower on disk: the indexes the engine keeps by itself since #234, for
which ADR-0075 published 9% on a transition in memory, and nothing else
of the phase. That and the rest the measuring found are under [What the
measuring found](#what-the-measuring-found).

The large database is the one Phase 8's records were measured on:
3,400,000 invoices, ten million versions and ten million moves through
draft, review and paid, a file of 7.6 GB with its copy of 0.62 GB
beside it. Globex has half the invoices and C-7 1,700 of them; each of
the two is a customer document that keeps the total of its invoices.

  took 0.53 ms.
- **A hundred agents that wait fall twice as far behind at p99**, 647
  ms against 322 ms in v0.7.0, one run of each; not looked into.
- **Lineage of a small customer takes a tenth longer** than when it was
  built, 65 to 67 ms against 60 to 61 ms; not looked into.
- **Two things have no benchmark**: a value that follows the clock
  (ADR-0093), whose record published 48 µs a document and a look of
  250 ms over 500,000, and a sign-in with a passkey (ADR-0088), whose
  record says one signature, well under a millisecond. Neither was
  measured here.
- **Every benchmark of the repository was run once**, to find one that
  fails while the gate says ok: on main, with a disk for those that need one, all 106 of the core and the 5 of the analytical module ran, and none failed or was skipped.
- **The measuring itself.** The disk confirmed a write of 4 KB in 0.36
  to 0.48 ms at 00:58, 01:13, 03:52 and 04:32, and in 3.7 to 4.1 ms at
  01:51, 03:20, 04:44, 04:56 and 05:08: each slow figure came within
  minutes of a run that wrote gigabytes. A commit does not wait for the
  disk. Nothing else measured meanwhile.

## What is slow

- **A caller on this machine waits 0.3 ms for the server** where the
  Go API answers in 0.06 ms, and most of that is the machine's.
- **Callers that write at once wait for each other.** There is one
  writer at a time; sixteen get about 9,000 commits a second through
  the server between them, each waiting 1.6 ms.
- **A commit costs a tenth more than in v0.7.0** for the indexes the
  engine keeps: a transition 0.063 ms at the median on disk, 12,800 a
  second.
- **A question that two indexes fit looks into each first**, a
  statement each: under a read rule over an indexed owner, one ticket
  by its number takes 23 µs, where it takes 16 µs without the rule.
- **Without the copy, a question that reads everything takes seconds**
  over 3,400,000 invoices: the flow with the spread of its stays 40 s,
  the flow alone 18 s, the paths 9 s, made and finished 8.5 s, the
  documents behind a number 2.4 to 7 s, totals by owner and state 3.3
  s. The copy answers each in 6 to 540 ms.
- **Where a kept total over 1,700,000 documents comes from takes 21.5
  s**, from the store in both programs: it reads each document as it
  was.
- **A live `LOOKUP` keyed by a value half the class holds takes 0.6 s**
  without the copy, since #287 narrows it through the index.
- **A hundred agents that wait for every change fall behind** a writer
  that moves a document every millisecond: 43 ms at the median, 647 ms
  at p99. One agent waits 0.5 ms.
- **The stream falls behind a writer that never pauses** when a
  hundred subscribers listen: 11.1 ms at p99.
- **The flow of a class of a million documents takes four seconds**
  from the store for a caller who reads them all.
- **A value that follows a table costs 40 µs a document it brings**: a
  change that brings 100,000 takes 4 s.
- **Bringing a table's 5,000,000 rows in takes 15 s.**
- **Opening takes 0.7 ms a class**, and an open database of fifty
  classes holds 25 MB, and about 47 MB more once many callers have read
  from it at once, until it is closed.
- **Checking a password takes 60 ms of one core**, which is what it is
  for: a guess costs as much.
